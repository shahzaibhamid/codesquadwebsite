Multi-Agent Robotic Search Team

Reinforcement learning for robot search teams that must cope with unknown areas and failing members

This is research, not a client project. It comes from our founder's work in AI and robotics and was published in the Mehran University Research Journal of Engineering & Technology (2021).

The problem

Search and rescue puts people at risk, and robots fail too.

Disasters such as fires and building collapses are dangerous for rescue workers and dogs. Robots can search in their place, but most robots used in the field today are tele-operated.

Autonomous teams face three hard problems: the area is unknown, some areas are hazardous, and individual robots can run low on fuel, lose their connection or be destroyed.

The authors found that earlier models handled some of these (uncertainty, coordination, learning, fault detection) but not all together. The question behind this research: can a team learn good search behaviour and keep working when a member fails?

The approach

A leader, its members, and a learned policy.

The search area is split into partitions, each divided into blocks. A leader agent assigns members to partitions and manages their modes. Each partition is unknown, friendly or hostile, and that status is only discovered by searching.

Both levels are modelled as Markov Decision Processes. The leader tracks agent status, goal status, partition assignment, partition status and standby agents. Members move block to block (left, right, straight, back, stay) while tracking fuel, faults and hazards.

Q-learning is then applied on top of the MDP to build a Q lookup table, so each member can act on its own with less dependence on the leader. A member that stops responding is treated as faulty and put on standby, and the leader keeps trying to reconnect or sends another agent.

Key capabilities

What the work covers.

Fault tolerance

Non-responding agents are detected and moved to standby

Learning on the agent

Members decide from their own feedback, not only the leader

Flexible scale

The number of agents and search partitions can be changed

Read this carefully

What these results do and don't show.

All results come from simulations on small grids (for example three-by-three partitions), with policies computed in MATLAB 2013a. The paper reports no field tests or real-robot deployment.

The comparison is by scenario and by qualitative properties. The paper does not report a single accuracy or speed-up figure, so this page does not either.
